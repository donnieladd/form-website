Storyboard Frames · editor reference, not slides
Camera
  • Phone, rear camera, 4K at 30fps (1080p at 30 is fine). Vertical.
  • Lock exposure and focus on your face before the take (tap and hold).
  • Phone at eye level on a stand or propped on books. Not handheld for the A-roll.
  • Frame: chest up, eyes on the upper-third line, a hand's width of headroom. Leave the bottom quarter empty for captions.
Light
  • One window, 45 degrees to your face, you facing it. Overhead lights off.
  • Golden hour is a bonus, not a requirement. If it is night, one lamp behind the phone, bounced off a wall.
  • Background: your real workspace. A screen with HQ on it is good if nothing private shows. No server-room imagery, no neon.
Sound
  • Quiet room, fans off, phone within three feet of your mouth. AirPods as a mic are acceptable; a lav is better.
  • Record five seconds of silence before the first take (room tone for the edit).
  • Say the last word of each beat, then stop for one full second. That silence is what lets the editor cut cleanly.
Wardrobe
  • Dark solid top, like the portrait. No logos, no patterns, no white.
Performance
  • Say it, don't read it. The script is the shape; your words are the words.
  • Take 1: clean, unhurried. Take 2: 15 percent faster. Take 3: only the hook, three ways.
  • No forced energy. Direct eye contact. The authority is in the thought, not the delivery.
  • Profanity is fine if it is yours. Exclamation points are not.
Edit
  • Cut the pauses, keep the breaths. No hyperactive jump cuts; one cut per beat is plenty.
  • Captions on, auto-generated, then corrected. Satoshi Bold white if the app allows; otherwise the cleanest sans it has.
  • On-screen lines from the storyboard, one per beat, placed between 25 and 70 percent of the frame height. The italic word in signal blue #3D6BFF if the app lets you color a word; otherwise all white.
  • Cover: use c2-reel-cover.jpg. End card: the last storyboard frame for 2 seconds.
  • No music under the voice. If you want a bed, keep it under minus 24 dB and stop it before the CTA.
Export
  • 1080 × 1920, H.264, 30fps, highest quality. Under 60 seconds. Export once, post the same file everywhere.

The Authority Path, in Plain English

Dontae
Caption · from Dontae
The dangerous AI systems aren't the smart ones. They're the ones that can grant themselves permission.

Here is the most important line in form. | HQ, in plain English. An AI employee can recommend an action. It cannot grant itself permission to take it.

Every meaningful action walks the same path. A request arrives, from a person, from Slack, from another employee. It becomes one standard record, the same shape every time, so nothing downstream has to guess. That record is routed to the employee who owns the work. The employee thinks, with its own context, memory, skills, and tools. Then, before anything touches the world, the action meets a deterministic authority check and, where policy requires it, a human approval gate. Whatever happens next is recorded, costed, and traced.

Deterministic means no model is involved in that decision. It is data: who may do what, with what, until when.

Why this matters if you run an organization: the moment an AI can act without a rule you wrote, you no longer run it.

That one boundary is the difference between an assistant and an employee you can hold accountable. This part is built. The approval desk is in the console today.

Music Taught Me How to Build a Room

Dontae
Caption · from Dontae
People think I left music for tech. I didn't leave anything.

A session is a system. You arrange parts so a room full of people can execute one outcome, live, on time, and recover when someone comes in early. Rehearsal is architecture. The click track is a contract everyone agrees to so nobody has to hold the time alone.

I didn't know that was systems thinking. I thought it was producing.

Now I design workflows where people and AI employees hand work to each other, and the questions are the same ones. Who has the downbeat. What happens when a part drops. How does the room recover without stopping.

If you've ever led worship, led a band, a team, or a room, you already know more about systems than you think.

Looking back, I've been building pieces of this my whole career. I just found its technical language. The parallels are in the slides. Follow along and join me.
